What * meant in my pattern:
In the pattern data*.txt, the asterisk (*) acted as a wildcard matching any character sequence between "data" and ".txt". This allowed the shell to instantly group data1.txt, data2.txt, and data3.txt together.

Why I listed matches before copying:
Listing matches first with ls allows me to inspect exactly which files the wildcard expansion expands to before executing a modification command. This verification step ensures I do not accidentally operate on unintended files that happen to share a similar naming pattern.

Why wildcards are dangerous with delete commands:
Wildcards expand automatically before the command executes, meaning a misplaced asterisk or typo like rm * .txt instead of rm *.txt can permanently wipe entire directories. Because terminal delete operations bypass the trash bin and cannot be undone easily, running unverified wildcard deletions carries a high risk of catastrophic data loss.
